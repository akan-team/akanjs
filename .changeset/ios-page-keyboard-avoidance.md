An iOS app's page now meets the keyboard the way a native screen does: its content ends at the keys and moves with them, and a header stays where it is. In keyboard mode "none" (what akan apps ship) WKWebView still met the keyboard on its own, insetting its scroll view and panning the whole page until the focused field cleared the keys, so the header went off screen, the page showed under the status bar, and whatever the frame did for the keyboard came on top of the pan. The iOS shell now takes the keyboard notifications away from WKWebView in "none", as Capacitor's Keyboard plugin does, and tells the page through `__AKAN_NATIVE__.keyboardAvoidance = "page"`. Under such a shell the frame ends every page's content at the keys (not only a page with a keyboard accessory), drops the bottom safe area and inset the keys cover, animates on the duration the shell reports with UIKit's keyboard curve (`IOS_KEYBOARD_ANIMATION_EASING`), and scrolls a field the keys cover back into view; a dialog ends at the keys too, `useVisualViewportFrame` reports the keys from the shell, `KeyboardAvoiding` steps aside, and `--akan-keyboard-duration` / `--akan-keyboard-easing` plus `data-akan-keyboard` (while the keys are up) on the root let a page move its own parts with the keys. A page under an older shell, which still pans, keeps today's behavior.

The api report lists additions only: `Device.keyboardAnimationMs`, `Device.keyboardHeight` and `Device.subscribeKeyboard`, the optional `KeyboardFrameState.avoidsContent` and `isPageKeyboardAvoidance` from `akanjs/webkit`; no upgrade step is needed. An iOS app gets the new behavior once it is built with this shell; a page that lifted its own composer by the keyboard's height under the old pan should drop that lift.
