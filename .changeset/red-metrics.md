Every endpoint and page counts its requests and times them, always, with tracing left off.

- `akan_endpoint_requests_total{endpoint, status_class}` and `akan_endpoint_request_duration_seconds{endpoint}` count and time each endpoint call over HTTP, websocket and MCP, labelled `<type>:<key>` as a trace names it. `akan_page_requests_total{route, status_class}` and `akan_page_request_duration_seconds{route}` do the same for page documents, a `?csr=true` shell included, and RSC payloads by route id, and a path no route matches counts as `unmatched`. Every series also carries the process `role`. No label carries a URL, a user, an account or a query.
- `akan_route_cache_lookups_total{cache, outcome, reason}` counts the route caches' lookups: `cache` is `rsc`, counted in the render worker, or `html`, counted in the web server, and `outcome` is a `hit` or `miss` whose `reason` says whether the key took the query as requested or rewritten, or a `bypass` whose `reason` says why the request skipped the cache. The Grafana dashboard shows each cache's hit ratio and its bypasses by reason.
- A latency histogram has 12 fixed buckets from 1 ms to 10 s. A label takes at most 2,000 values, and the rest count as `other`. A series appears once it is first observed, at about 2 KB of memory for each endpoint called.
- `/_akan/app/metrics` answers these series as `metricFamilies`. Behind a gateway, each replica reports its own with its start time, and the gateway adds them up: counters and buckets add, and percentiles are never averaged. A replica that restarts counts from zero again, and the gateway keeps its earlier counts in the sum, so the sum never drops. A series the RSC worker registers reaches its server's scrape the same way, and a recycled or restarted worker does not lower it.
- `/_akan/app/metrics` answers a Prometheus scrape as text, behind the same token. `Accept: application/openmetrics-text`, which Prometheus sends, gets OpenMetrics 1.0, and `text/plain` alone gets the Prometheus 0.0.4 text; `*/*`, `application/json` or no Accept keeps the JSON. Both carry `akan_build_info{akanjs_version, app}`, `process_start_time_seconds` and the RSC worker series: `akan_rsc_workers`, `akan_rsc_workers_ready`, `akan_rsc_renders_pending`, `akan_rsc_sends_queued`, `akan_rsc_worker_resident_memory_bytes`, `akan_rsc_worker_restarts_total` and `akan_rsc_worker_recycles_total`.
- A metrics sample no longer walks the JSC heap with `heapStats()`, which stopped the event loop for about 46 ms on a 150 MiB heap at every sample. The `jsc…` fields (`jscHeapSizeBytes`, `jscObjectCount` and the rest, and their `rscWorker…` copies) come only while memory is diagnosed, under `AKAN_MEMORY_LOG=1` or `AKAN_MEMORY_GC_ON_REPORT=1`.

**Upgrading.** Most readers change nothing. The metrics JSON gains a top-level `metricFamilies`, and each replica's `metrics` gains `startedAt` and `metricFamilies`; a reader that checks the body's exact keys must allow them. Behind a gateway the sums are as fresh as each replica's last report, sent every `AKAN_MEMORY_LOG_INTERVAL_MS` (60 s by default). Set it to your scrape interval for fresher numbers. A reader that sends `Accept: text/plain` alone now gets the text; send `application/json` to keep the JSON. The response carries `Vary: Accept`. A reader of the `jsc…` fields sets `AKAN_MEMORY_LOG=1`; `heapUsedBytes` stays in every sample.
