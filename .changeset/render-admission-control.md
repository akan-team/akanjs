A render worker can run a set number of renders at a time, keep the rest in a first-come, first-served queue, and answer 503 once that queue is full. It does so only when `AKAN_RSC_WORKER_MAX_CONCURRENCY` is set.

- `AKAN_RSC_WORKER_MAX_CONCURRENCY=<n>` lets one render worker run `n` renders at once: page documents, `/__rsc` navigations and prefetches, and the error or not-found page rendered in a failed render's place. The rest wait in the order they arrived. A page the worker has stored is replayed without a slot, also when another request stored it while this one waited; a request its client aborted leaves the queue without rendering, and a slot comes back when its render ends, throws or is cancelled. A render that has held its slot as long as the idle timeout gives it back and runs on. The cap is per worker, so per replica behind a gateway, and it bounds the renders admitted, not the ones still running after their slot came back: a page whose client left keeps running until its own `await` settles. MCP page prompts and static app cards are not queued. A value that is not a whole number or `off` is warned about once and ignored.
- `AKAN_RSC_WORKER_MAX_QUEUE` bounds the queue: by default the cap times the idle timeout's seconds (`AKAN_IDLE_TIMEOUT`, 120), and `0` or `off` for no bound. A render past it, or one that has waited as long as the idle timeout (the default 120 seconds where that timeout is off, as under `akan start`), is answered `503` with `Retry-After: 1` and `Cache-Control: no-store` before any of it renders: for a browser a page that reloads itself once, after a second plus jitter and twice as long after each refusal in a row up to 30 seconds; `base.error.serverUnavailable` JSON for a JSON caller; and text otherwise and for `/__rsc`. The error codes are `renderQueueFull` and `renderQueueTimeout`.
- `/_akan/app/metrics` gains `akan_rsc_renders_in_flight`, `akan_rsc_render_queue_depth`, `akan_rsc_renders_abandoned` (renders still running after their slot came back), `akan_rsc_render_queue_wait_seconds` and `akan_rsc_render_refusals_total{reason}` (`queue-full`, `wait-timeout`, and `hold-timeout` for a slot taken back). A refused page also counts as a `5xx` of its route in `akan_page_requests_total`. A page that waited carries `queueMs` on its request line and, under `AKAN_TRACE=1`, a `renderQueue` span.
- The cap is off by default, because a page that awaits data holds its slot while it waits. Measured on the production build of `apps/akan` with 32 requests at once (`pkgs/@akanjs/devkit/integration/renderAdmissionSoak.ts`, the median of three runs): pages that wait 20–200 ms on data served 151 requests a second with no cap, 150 with a cap of 32, 95 with 16, 59 with 8 and 32 with 4. Pages that only use the CPU served 102 to 108 a second at every cap, and their median latency moved by less than a tenth (250 ms at a cap of 4, 264 ms with none): the worker starts and finishes renders in order, but while it stays busy its payloads reach the web server together, once it pauses, so a cap bounds a burst and does not answer the first pages sooner. What a cap of 4 changed for them was the worker's heap growth during a run: 54 to 64 MB, against 82 to 284 MB with none. `pkgs/akanjs/server/SSR_MEMORY_DIAGNOSIS.md` has the table.

**Upgrading.** Nothing changes until `AKAN_RSC_WORKER_MAX_CONCURRENCY` is set; three new gauges appear in the metrics scrape. When you set it, start from the number of requests one replica is sized to hold at once, so the cap only bounds a burst past that, and go lower only for pages that render without waiting on data. A capped server answers `503` with `Retry-After` under overload, so let the load balancer pass that status through uncached, and treat it as retryable in an uptime check.
