The `external-view` plugin now works on Linux, on Wayland as well as X11. A view lies over the window's page at its bounds, with a browser profile of its own; its top level stays on the origins it was opened for while its frames load web content from anywhere, and its init script runs in the top document only. A failed load off those origins shows no error page there, a page's `window.close()` leaves the view in place, a hidden view stays hidden when the window is shown again, and an unattended app's views open no file chooser or print dialog. `capture` returns a PNG of the view's content, and `setMuted`, `clearData`, `loadFailed` (`http<status>`, `cannotConnect`, `hostNameNotResolved`, `timeout`, `certificate`, …) and `gone` (`crashed`, `killed` or `oom`; the view loads its page again by itself) work as on Windows. The visible view shown last holds the keyboard focus inside its window — on open, when shown, after a navigation, a reload or a restart, and when the window is focused again — and the page takes it back when the view is hidden or closed. Linux sends no `consoleMessage`: the `console` option is accepted and does nothing there. A plugin's `desktop` entry can now leave methods out of one desktop OS with `"unsupported": { "linux": [...] }`, so `isSupported()` answers per OS: `external-view` now reports `sendKey`, `setMuted` and `capture` unsupported on macOS, where the shell already refused them.
